Menopause Guide

Nutrition strategies to support your health during menopause: the key nutrients, balanced meals, foods to limit and staying hydrated.

Download the full guide (PDF)~20 MB
1. Why nutrition matters

Menopause is a time of significant change, not just hormonally but also in how the body processes and needs nutrients. As estrogen levels decline, your nutritional needs shift, and what you eat plays a crucial role in supporting your overall well-being.

Nutrition can help manage many symptoms of menopause, from hot flashes and mood swings to bone loss and weight changes. A well-balanced diet can be a powerful tool for physical health and emotional well-being during this transition.

By focusing on key nutrients, making balanced food choices and avoiding dietary triggers, you can ease the physical and emotional challenges of menopause. Certain foods can balance your hormones, strengthen your bones and help maintain a healthy metabolism.

2. How hormonal changes affect your needs

Menopause brings a decline in estrogen and progesterone, which directly affects how your body processes and absorbs nutrients. As estrogen drops, absorption of calcium and vitamin D decreases, raising the risk of bone density loss. Magnesium and vitamin B6 also become harder to absorb efficiently.

Focus on

  • Calcium-rich foods: dairy, fortified plant-based products and leafy greens for bone strength.
  • Vitamin D: from sunlight, fortified foods or supplements, to aid calcium absorption.
  • Magnesium-rich foods: nuts, seeds and whole grains to support muscle and nerve function.

Slowing metabolism and weight

Hormonal changes can slow metabolism, and the body may store more fat around the stomach even with similar eating and activity habits.

  • Portion control: balanced portions of vegetables, fruits and lean proteins.
  • Regular exercise: strength training and cardio to support metabolism and maintain muscle.
  • Fibre-rich foods: whole grains, legumes, fruits and vegetables for digestion and fullness.
Top tipEating smaller, balanced meals regularly through the day may help keep blood sugar stable, supporting energy and mood. Combining protein with fibre-rich foods promotes fullness and reduces cravings.

Bone health

Estrogen helps maintain bone strength and density, so bone loss risk rises as it declines, which may contribute to osteoporosis.

  • Calcium: yogurt, cheese and fortified plant milks.
  • Vitamin D: sunlight, salmon, fortified cereals, or supplements.
  • Magnesium: dark leafy greens, almonds and legumes.
Top tipCombine weight-bearing exercise and resistance training with a calcium and vitamin D-rich diet to help maintain bone density.

Heart health

Estrogen helps regulate cholesterol. As it declines, LDL (“bad”) cholesterol may rise and HDL (“good”) may fall, potentially raising heart disease risk.

  • Heart-healthy foods: whole grains, healthy fats (avocado, nuts, seeds) and lean proteins.
  • Omega-3s: salmon, flaxseeds and chia seeds.
  • Reduce processed foods: minimise processed sugars, trans fats and refined carbs.
Top tipInclude olive oil, nuts and avocado as part of a balanced diet. With regular activity, they may help support healthy cholesterol.

Balancing hormones through food

  • More fruits and vegetables for better nutrition overall.
  • Fewer sugars and refined carbs to help keep blood sugar and hormone levels balanced.
  • Healthy fats like olive oil, avocado and fatty fish to support hormone balance.
3. Key nutrients
Calcium
~1,200 mg/day

Essential for strong bones, especially when bone loss risk increases after menopause.

Best sources: Dairy (milk, yogurt, cheese), fortified plant milks, leafy greens (kale, broccoli), fortified orange juice.

Vitamin D

Needed to absorb calcium. Also supports the immune system and helps regulate mood.

Best sources: Sunlight, fatty fish (salmon, mackerel), fortified cereals, supplements.

Magnesium
~320 mg/day

Supports muscle and nerve function, energy and bones. May ease insomnia and anxiety.

Best sources: Nuts (almonds, cashews), seeds, whole grains, leafy greens, legumes.

Omega-3 fatty acids
Several times a week

Support heart health, may help reduce inflammation, lower blood pressure and balance cholesterol.

Best sources: Fatty fish (salmon, sardines), flaxseeds, chia seeds, walnuts.

Vitamin B6
~1.5 mg/day

Helps regulate mood, may reduce irritability and fatigue, and supports serotonin production.

Best sources: Poultry (chicken, turkey), fish (tuna, salmon), bananas, fortified cereals.

Phytoestrogens

Natural compounds that mimic estrogen. May help ease hot flashes and night sweats.

Best sources: Soy products (tofu, tempeh, soy milk), flaxseeds, sesame seeds, legumes.

Iron
~8 mg/day

Needs fall after menstruation ends, but iron still matters for oxygen transport and avoiding fatigue.

Best sources: Lean meats, beans, lentils, spinach, fortified cereals.

A nutrient-rich diet may also reduce the long-term risk of osteoporosis and cardiovascular disease, which become more common as estrogen declines.

4. Practical meal strategies

Balanced meals with healthy fats, fibre and whole grains can help support hormonal balance. Even small changes can make a difference to daily energy and mood.

Sample day

  • Breakfast: oatmeal topped with flaxseeds and berries (fibre, antioxidants and phytoestrogens).
  • Lunch: leafy greens like spinach or kale with grilled salmon, pumpkin seeds and a drizzle of olive oil.
  • Dinner: stir-fry with tofu or tempeh and broccoli or Brussels sprouts, served with quinoa.

Snack ideas

  • Fruit and nuts: berries with a handful of mixed nuts.
  • Yogurt with flaxseeds: probiotics for gut health plus phytoestrogens.
  • Nuts and seeds: almonds or walnuts for healthy fats and protein.

Meal timing and blood sugar

  • Eat regular meals: don’t skip meals. Aim for balanced meals every 3–4 hours.
  • Focus on protein: include a protein source with each meal and snack for fullness and steady energy.
  • Stay hydrated: water supports metabolism and how the body responds to hormonal changes.

Anti-inflammatory foods

Inflammation may contribute to joint discomfort and mood changes. Foods that may help:

  • Ginger and turmeric: add to teas, soups and stir-fries.
  • Leafy greens: kale and spinach are rich in antioxidants.
  • Omega-3 fish: salmon and mackerel also support heart and brain health.
5. Managing weight and metabolism

Metabolism naturally slows during menopause due to hormonal changes, reduced muscle mass and changes in energy expenditure. Declining estrogen can also shift fat storage towards the abdomen. These changes are normal, but they call for a more mindful, balanced approach.

Pro tipA brisk daily walk or more fibre can support metabolism and healthy weight. Consistency matters more than intensity.

Eat nutrient-dense foods

Cutting calories doesn’t mean skipping meals or restricting nutrients.

  • Fruits and vegetables: fibre, vitamins and antioxidants that support fullness and digestion.
  • Lean proteins: fish, chicken, beans and tofu help maintain muscle and reduce overeating.
  • Whole grains: brown rice, quinoa, oats and wholemeal products give steady energy.
FactSkipping meals may negatively affect metabolism and increase the chance of overeating later in the day.

Stay active

  • Cardio: walking, swimming and cycling support heart health and daily energy use.
  • Strength training: light weights, resistance bands, squats and lunges help preserve muscle, bones and mobility.

Drink about eight glasses of water per day, or more depending on activity, to support metabolism and fullness.

6. Foods to limit

Some foods and drinks may worsen symptoms such as hot flashes, poor sleep, energy dips and weight changes. Simple adjustments, like cutting back on processed sugars, spicy foods and alcohol, may help.

FactKeep a symptom journal. Tracking meals alongside daily symptoms and energy can help you spot your own triggers.
Processed and sugary foods

Can cause rapid blood sugar swings that affect energy, mood and cravings, and are often low in nutrients.

Examples: sweets, pastries, packaged snacks, sugary cereals.

Swap: fruit, nuts or homemade energy bars.

Caffeine

May contribute to hot flashes, night sweats and anxiety in some women, and can disrupt sleep.

Examples: coffee, energy drinks, black tea, some sodas.

Swap: cut back, especially in the afternoon and evening. Try herbal or decaffeinated drinks.

Alcohol

Can increase hot flashes, interfere with sleep, contribute to weight gain and worsen mood swings or anxiety.

Examples: wine, beer, cocktails, spirits.

Swap: limit or avoid it. Try sparkling water with fresh citrus.

Spicy foods

May trigger hot flashes and raise body temperature in some women. Limiting very spicy foods may help if you’re sensitive.

7. Hydration and wellness

Hot flashes and night sweats can increase fluid loss, and dehydration may intensify fatigue, headaches and dry skin.

  • Regulates body temperature: water supports natural cooling, which may help during hot flashes and night sweats.
  • Supports digestion: may help reduce constipation.
  • Maintains energy: dehydration can contribute to low energy and fatigue.

Hydration beyond water

  • Herbal teas: chamomile, peppermint or ginger, caffeine-free.
  • Water-rich foods: cucumber, watermelon and oranges.
  • Broths and soups: vegetable or bone broth also provide sodium and potassium.

Caffeine and alcohol may contribute to dehydration and make symptoms harder to manage. If you drink caffeine, consider increasing water.

Signs you may be dehydrated

  • Dry skin
  • Fatigue or low energy
  • Headaches or dizziness
  • Dark yellow urine

Simple ways to stay hydrated

  • Carry a reusable water bottle.
  • Set reminders on your phone.
  • Flavour water with lemon, cucumber or fresh mint.
  • Drink a glass of water before meals.
Hydration tipAim for at least eight glasses of water a day, and more in hot weather or when you’re more active.
8. Key takeaways
  • Stay hydrated: supports temperature regulation, energy and less fatigue.
  • Focus on key nutrients: calcium, vitamin D, magnesium and healthy fats for bones, hormones and wellness.
  • Avoid common triggers: caffeine, alcohol, processed sugars and high-sodium foods may worsen hot flashes and bloating.
  • Eat balanced, nutrient-dense meals: whole foods to support energy, mood and metabolism.
  • Be patient: meaningful progress takes time, and your needs may keep changing. Small, consistent steps add up.

Inflammation Fix

The 7-step guide to cooling inflammation, healing your gut and losing stubborn fat for good.

Download the full guide (PDF)~2 MB

Why you’re stuck

You’ve been eating “healthy” and exercising, but the belly fat won’t budge, the bloating won’t stop and you’re exhausted. It’s not about eating less or moving more. It’s about putting out the fire inside your body.

What is inflammation?

Inflammation is your body’s natural defence system. When it’s constantly “on” from stress, poor food choices, lack of sleep and toxins, it becomes chronic. Chronic inflammation is linked to:

  • Stubborn belly fat
  • Bloating, puffiness and water retention
  • Brain fog and fatigue
  • Joint pain and stiffness
  • Hormonal imbalances

The good news: you can reverse it, starting today, with these 7 steps.

Step 1

Cut the crap

Sugar, seed oils and processed foods are gasoline on the fire.

Avoid
  • Packaged snacks
  • Fried foods
  • Canola, soybean and sunflower oil
  • Sweets, syrups and “diet” food
Instead
  • Whole foods
  • Olive oil, avocado, grass-fed meat
  • Fresh herbs and spices
Step 2

Stop snacking all day

Constant grazing means constantly elevated insulin, which means chronic inflammation.

  • Eat 3 satisfying meals
  • Leave 4–5 hours between meals
  • No eating 2 hours before bed
Step 3

Add anti-inflammatory foods

These foods help extinguish the fire. Daily must-haves:

  • Berries (especially blueberries)
  • Green tea or matcha
  • Leafy greens (spinach, kale, rocket)
  • Cruciferous veggies (broccoli, cauliflower, cabbage)
  • Extra virgin olive oil
Step 4

Hydrate like it’s your job

Dehydration traps toxins and slows detox.

Target: 2.5 to 3L of water a day. Add a pinch of sea salt or electrolytes for better absorption.

Step 5

Heal your gut

Bad gut means bad inflammation, which means stubborn weight. Start here:

  • 1 tbsp apple cider vinegar in water before meals
  • Fermented foods (sauerkraut, kimchi, kefir)
  • Go gluten-free and dairy-free for 14 days
  • Chew your food properly
Step 6

Sleep deep

Poor sleep means higher cortisol, which means more inflammation. Do this nightly:

  • Magnesium glycinate
  • No screens 1 hour before bed
  • Cool, pitch-black room
  • Chamomile or peppermint tea
Step 7

Walk + breathe daily

Movement and mindfulness calm the nervous system. Non-negotiables:

  • 20-minute walk (bonus: barefoot outside)
  • 3 rounds of box breathing (4–4–4–4)
  • Sunlight in your eyes first thing in the morning

1-day anti-inflammatory meal plan

A template to fuel your body. Pick one option for each meal.

Morning ritual
  • Warm water with 1 tbsp apple cider vinegar
  • 10 deep breaths in sunlight
  • 1–2 cups warm water with lemon
Breakfast (pick one)
  • Eggs & greens: 2 whole eggs + 3 egg whites scrambled in olive oil, ½ avocado, sauteed spinach, green tea.
  • Chia protein pudding: 3 tbsp chia soaked in almond milk overnight, with cinnamon, a scoop of protein and berries, plus 1 boiled egg.
  • Greek yogurt bowl: ¾ cup unsweetened full-fat Greek yogurt with ½ cup blueberries.
Lunch (pick one)
  • Salmon power bowl: 120–150g grilled salmon, mixed greens with olive oil and apple cider vinegar, ½ cup cooked quinoa, cucumber, beetroot and red onion.
  • Turkey lettuce wraps: 120g turkey breast, lettuce leaves with hummus and shredded carrot, 1 boiled egg, and a cup of bone broth.
  • Tuna + avocado salad: tuna in olive oil, ½ avocado, cherry tomatoes, rocket and hemp seeds.
Afternoon snack (optional)
  • Handful of berries + 10 almonds
  • Low-carb protein shake
  • Boiled egg + celery
Dinner (pick one)
  • Stir fry: grass-fed beef strips, broccoli, zucchini and capsicum in coconut oil, with cauliflower rice.
  • Lemon baked salmon: 150g salmon with steamed asparagus and Brussels sprouts, drizzled with olive oil.
  • Chicken soup: broth-based soup with shredded chicken, carrot, celery and zucchini, spiced with turmeric and pepper.
Evening wind-down
  • Magnesium glycinate
  • Herbal tea
  • No screens after 8pm
  • Light stretching or journaling

Stick to this guide for 14 days. No perfection needed, just consistency. You’ll see:

  • Less bloating and puffiness
  • A flatter belly
  • Better sleep and energy
  • A clearer mind and mood
  • Easier fat loss
